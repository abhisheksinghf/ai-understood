## 1. Learn a distribution, not one correct output

A **discriminative** model might predict a poster's genre from its pixels: $p(\text{genre}\mid\text{image})$. A **generative** model describes how data can vary and produces samples from a learned distribution, such as $p_\theta(\text{image})$ or $p_\theta(\text{image}\mid\text{prompt})$. The parameters $\theta$ capture learned regularities; a random draw supplies variation.

**Training** adjusts those parameters using examples and an objective. **Sampling**, also called generation, uses the resulting model to produce an output. A new seed normally changes the sampled variation without retraining the model.

For our movie poster, the prompt is a **condition**: “quiet seaside scene, cool colors, plenty of empty space.” Conditions can also be class labels, another image, audio, or layout constraints. A condition steers a distribution; it is not a guarantee that every detail will be obeyed.

Sampling is different from retrieving a stored picture, although models can memorize or reproduce training material. Neither novelty nor originality follows automatically from calling an output “generated.”

## 2. Choose what the model represents

The same output can be represented in different ways. A pixel array is direct but large; a compressed **latent** representation can be smaller. A latent coordinate is not automatically a human-readable concept such as “calmness.”

| Output | Possible representation | What must stay consistent? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Movie poster | Pixels, patches, or compressed image codes | Objects, layout, colors, and any lettering |
| Trailer soundtrack | Waveform samples, spectrograms, or audio codec tokens | Rhythm, timbre, and continuity |
| Trailer video | Frames or compressed spatial and temporal features | Identity and motion across time |
| Other data | Tabular rows, 3D points, meshes, or discrete symbols | Valid structure and domain constraints |

**Representation, architecture, objective, and sampler are separate choices.** A transformer is an architecture; it can predict the next token or participate in a denoising system. “Diffusion” names a modeling approach, not a requirement to use one particular network architecture.

Compression trades detail against cost. A decoder cannot reliably restore information its representation has discarded. Evaluate the final decoded output, not just a loss measured in latent space.

## 3. Variational autoencoders: learn a space to sample

A **variational autoencoder (VAE)** pairs an encoder with a decoder. The encoder maps an example to a distribution over latent variables, often described by a mean $\mu$ and standard deviation $\sigma$. It samples $z=\mu+\sigma\odot\epsilon$, with $\epsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,I)$, then the decoder reconstructs the example.

Training balances reconstruction with a penalty encouraging the encoded distribution to resemble a chosen prior. For a new poster, sample $z$ from that prior and decode it; no input poster is required. Reconstruction starts from an existing example; generation starts from a prior draw. See [Auto-Encoding Variational Bayes](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.6114).

<details className="optional-depth">
<summary>Go deeper: the VAE objective and reparameterization</summary>

A standard negative evidence lower bound is

$$L=\mathbb E_{q_\phi(z\mid x)}[-\log p_\theta(x\mid z)]+D_{KL}(q_\phi(z\mid x)\Vert p(z)).$$

The first term rewards explaining the example; KL regularizes the latent distribution. Reparameterization separates the random draw from differentiable encoder outputs, letting gradients reach the encoder. Implementations often predict log variance for numerical convenience. Strong reconstruction pressure and strong regularization can compete; low loss does not guarantee sharp or diverse images. An ordinary autoencoder lacks this explicit prior-matching objective, so decoding arbitrary random codes need not produce useful samples.

</details>

## 4. GANs: learn through an adversary

A **generative adversarial network (GAN)** trains two networks together. The generator transforms random noise into synthetic examples. The discriminator learns to distinguish real training examples from generated ones. Its feedback teaches the generator which statistical differences remain detectable.

For movie posters, a discriminator might initially exploit strange textures or broken shapes. As the generator changes, the discriminator's task also changes. At generation time, the basic GAN uses the generator alone: the discriminator is not a mandatory approval gate. See the [original GAN paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.2661).

<details className="optional-depth">
<summary>Go deeper: mode collapse and unstable training</summary>

**Mode collapse** means the generator covers too little of the real distribution. It might produce attractive posters that all share almost the same composition, even when the examples contain many styles. Sharpness alone would miss this failure.

Because the networks learn against changing opponents, their losses need not improve smoothly together. The idealized equilibrium in the original formulation is not a promise that finite networks and an optimizer will reach it. Practical objectives and regularization vary; evaluate sample variety and quality separately.

</details>

## 5. Autoregressive models: generate in an order

An autoregressive model factors a joint distribution into conditional predictions:

$$p(x_1,\ldots,x_n\mid c)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}p(x_i\mid x_{<i},c).$$

The elements could be text tokens, image codes, or audio tokens. At sampling time, each new prediction is conditioned on the generated prefix. A poster model might produce a sequence of compressed image codes that a decoder converts into pixels. [PixelCNN](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.05328) is an early example of autoregressive image modeling at the pixel level.

The ordering makes the probability calculation clear, but sequential generation can add latency and early mistakes can affect later choices. Training often uses known prefixes; generation must live with its own outputs. This is the same teacher-forcing distinction introduced in the language-model chapters.

<details className="optional-depth">
<summary>Go deeper: normalizing flows are another generative family</summary>

A **normalizing flow** transforms a simple random variable through invertible mappings: $x=f(z)$. Invertibility and a tractable Jacobian determinant allow a density calculation using change of variables:

$$\log p_X(x)=\log p_Z(f^{-1}(x))+\log\left|\det J_{f^{-1}}(x)\right|.$$

This trades architectural restrictions and computation against tractable density evaluation. Not every decoder is invertible, and not every model with “flow” in its name computes this discrete formula. **Flow matching**, below, is a training method for continuous velocity fields. The two ideas are related but not interchangeable. See [normalizing flows](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.05770) and the [flow-matching paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02747).

</details>

## 6. Diffusion training: learn to denoise

Diffusion adds noise to examples through a known forward process, then learns how to reverse the corruption statistically. In a common noise-prediction formulation, choose a timestep and construct

$$x_t=\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0+\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\epsilon,\qquad \epsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,I).$$

$x_0$ is clean data. $\bar\alpha_t$ is a cumulative signal coefficient between zero and one; smaller values mean stronger corruption. A network receives $x_t$, the timestep, and optionally a condition. It predicts noise, often using a weighted or simplified mean-squared-error objective against the noise used during training. Other parameterizations predict clean data or a velocity-like target.

The timestep matters: light corruption and nearly pure noise require different estimates. The network learns regularities across examples; it cannot generally identify the exact random noise that corrupted an unseen example. See [Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.11239).

## 7. Sampling: follow estimates toward data

Generation begins with a noise draw. A **sampler** repeatedly queries the learned denoiser and updates the current state toward a less noisy one. Different samplers use different update rules; some add fresh randomness and some are deterministic given the starting noise. The number of sampling steps is separate from the number of training updates.

**Latent diffusion** runs this process in a compressed representation, then decodes the final latent to an image. This can reduce computation relative to working on all pixels. It adds a dependency on the encoder and decoder: a good denoising result cannot compensate for every compression failure. See [High-Resolution Image Synthesis with Latent Diffusion Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.10752).

**Classifier-free guidance (CFG)** combines conditional and unconditional predictions. For the noise-prediction convention used here,

$$\hat\epsilon=\epsilon_u+s(\epsilon_c-\epsilon_u).$$

$s=0$ uses the unconditional prediction; $s=1$ uses the conditional prediction; larger values extrapolate the difference. This requires a model that can provide both predictions, commonly trained by sometimes dropping the condition. Stronger guidance can improve prompt alignment but reduce diversity or introduce artifacts. It is not a universal quality slider. See [Classifier-Free Diffusion Guidance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.12598).

<details className="optional-depth">
<summary>Go deeper: a denoising calculation and a deterministic sampler</summary>

Rearranging the corruption equation gives $\hat x_0=(x_t-\sqrt{1-a}\,\hat\epsilon)/\sqrt a$, where $a=\bar\alpha_t$. If $a=0.25$, $x_0=1$, and $\epsilon=2$, then $x_t=2.2321$. Using the true noise recovers 1. Using 1.5 instead gives approximately 1.8660. Noise-estimation error is multiplied by $\sqrt{(1-a)/a}=1.7321$ here; at high corruption the original is especially uncertain.

A deterministic DDIM-style step toward a larger coefficient $a'$ is

$$x_{\mathrm{next}}=\sqrt{a'}\,\hat x_0+\sqrt{1-a'}\,\hat\epsilon.$$

This is the zero-additional-noise case, not the universal diffusion update. Our lab uses this form with an analytic estimator. Finite steps approximate a trajectory; a single clean estimate is not the whole sampling process. See [Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.02502). Seeds support repeatable comparisons within a fixed implementation; they do not promise identical outputs across models, devices, or software versions.

</details>

## 8. Flow matching: learn a velocity field

Imagine a movie-poster sample as a point moving from noise toward a data distribution. **Flow matching** learns a vector field that tells each point how to move at each time. For one simple training path, pair noise $z$ with an example $x$:

$$x_t=(1-t)z+tx,\qquad v_{\mathrm{target}}=x-z.$$

Here $t=0$ means noise and $t=1$ means data: **the opposite direction to the diffusion corruption index above**. Many pairs can pass through similar intermediate positions. The learned field estimates the velocity given the current point, time, and condition; it does not receive a secret destination during generation.

To sample, start from noise and solve $dx/dt=v_\theta(x,t)$ numerically. A basic Euler step is $x_{t+\Delta t}=x_t+\Delta t\,v_\theta(x_t,t)$. The solver, step size, and learned field determine the approximation. A straight training path does not guarantee each sampled trajectory is straight or that one step is sufficient. See [Flow Matching for Generative Modeling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02747).

## 9. Evaluate what the samples actually do

Do not compare generative systems with one attractive poster. Fix a prompt set, sample multiple seeds, and inspect both common and difficult cases.

| Question | Movie-poster check |
| --- | --- |
| Fidelity | Are shapes and textures coherent? |
| Diversity and coverage | Do seeds and prompts produce meaningfully different layouts and styles? |
| Conditioning | Does the calm prompt produce the requested mood and composition? |
| Exact constraints | Is the title spelled correctly? Are requested objects present in the right number? |
| Stability | Does editing one detail accidentally change the rest? For video, do identity and motion persist? |
| Practical cost | What are latency, memory use, and failure rate at the chosen settings? |

Distribution-level metrics, similarity scores, and human judgments answer different questions. A single score can hide poor prompt following or missing styles. A lower denoising loss is not itself proof of better lettering or more useful designs.

Use representative data you are entitled to use; track its provenance and avoid exposing private material. For an actual person or voice, obtain the relevant consent. Realism does not establish that a depicted scene happened. Our poster examples are fictional design exercises, not evidence about real films or events.

The lab represents posters as **two numbers**, not pixels. It uses two known Gaussian style distributions, not a trained neural network. This makes the calculations inspectable while keeping the limitations visible.

<details className="optional-depth">
<summary>Go deeper: the analytic estimator and the toy distribution</summary>

Calm samples have mean $[-1.25,-0.75]$; adventure samples have mean $[1.25,0.75]$. Both have per-coordinate variance $v_0=0.25$. The unconditional distribution is an equal mixture. For a known style mean $\mu$ and corrupted point $y$, elementary Gaussian conditioning gives

$$\mathbb E[x_0\mid y,c]=\mu+\frac{\sqrt a\,v_0}{a v_0+1-a}(y-\sqrt a\,\mu).$$

The corresponding noise estimate is $(y-\sqrt a\,\mathbb E[x_0\mid y,c])/\sqrt{1-a}$. Without the style label, the lab averages the two posterior means using their probabilities given $y$. A posterior mean minimizes expected squared error; it need not equal the original point or look like a typical sample.

Generation starts from a standard-normal draw at $a=0.001$ and follows a finite cosine-spaced schedule to $a=1$. That starting distribution only approximates the toy forward process's terminal distribution. Even this exact analytic denoiser plus a finite sampler does not promise exact draws from the target distribution. No coordinate here measures visual quality.

</details>

## 12. Connect generation to multimodal systems

You can now distinguish reconstructing an input, predicting a sequence, transforming a latent variable, denoising a sample, and integrating a velocity field. These are different mechanisms for modeling and sampling distributions; they are not a ladder in which one family universally replaces every other.

Return to the movie assistant: a recommender ranks films, a retrieval system finds evidence, and a generative model might create a poster concept. A **multimodal system** can connect these roles across text, images, documents, audio, and video. Chapter 30 explains those connections, including what it means to understand an image versus generate one.
